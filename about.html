Countryside

The Longer Story

Ordinary faith, written slowly.

I grew up in a small town where faith was simply part of the furniture: present, unremarkable, and quietly sustaining. I was ordained at twenty-six, certain I understood the Gospel. By thirty, I had discovered how little I knew about how it actually worked in a real life.

For two decades I sat with people in hospital waiting rooms, at kitchen tables, through divorces and funerals and quiet crises of faith. I preached on Sundays and visited on Tuesdays. I learned that the questions people actually carry are rarely the ones they ask in church.

When I left pastoral ministry, my wife suggested I write down what I had learned. Not a theology textbook. Not a self-help manual. Just honest words for people trying to follow Jesus between the interruptions.

That is what Jojjy is — a personal publication of short, honest reflections. Short enough to read in a quiet moment. Long enough, I hope, to stay.

What Shapes This Writing

Three convictions I write from.

Conviction 01

Rooted in Scripture

These articles are not self-help dressed in religious language. They begin with the Bible, not as a proof-text, but as a living word that has walked beside me through every season described here.

Conviction 02

Grounded in everyday

Faith that cannot survive a sink of dishes or a difficult conversation at work is a brittle thing. I write about faith in the places where most of us actually live: the commute, the argument, the Tuesday afternoon.

Conviction 03

Honest before God

I am not interested in presenting a tidier version of faith than I actually have. God can be trusted with our doubts, our grief, and our half-finished prayers.
